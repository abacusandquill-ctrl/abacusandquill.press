The Flying Sandolo

A novel by MPK Jensen
Planned publication: December 18, 2026

The Flying Sandolo is an imaginative children's adventure set in a world of extraordinary weather, scientific curiosity, everyday absurdity, and unexpected enchantment.

The story follows Elyssa, an observant and inquisitive girl who has experienced the death of her mother and is discovering a world far larger, stranger, funnier, and more beautiful than she had imagined.

In the rain-soaked town of Plucords, an extraordinary environment has shaped ordinary life: architecture, transportation, school, meals, habits, and the natural world. Beyond the town lies another realm of possibility, where a flying boat opens the way to astonishing encounters with the sky.

The novel combines adventure, humor, friendship, natural science, mythology, and questions about what it means to be alive.

The experience of the story

A world to inhabit

Plucords is not simply a backdrop for adventure. Its inhabitants have developed a recognizable everyday life around the peculiarities of their environment. Food, weather, local customs, school routines, eccentric personalities, and the behavior of animals all contribute to the pleasure of spending time there.

The book offers both the intimacy of a richly inhabited place and the exhilaration of entering a marvelous world beyond it.

Its imaginative appeal includes coziness, comedy, sensory detail, discovery, and the feeling that life continues in its fictional world even when the central adventure pauses.

Science and enchantment

Natural science and fantasy coexist throughout the novel.

Mycology, ecology, meteorology, astronomy, and close observation of the physical world are integral to its imaginative life. Knowledge does not dispel wonder; understanding a phenomenon can make it more astonishing.

The sky is more than a destination or spectacle. Clouds, the moon, and a tiny blue star belong to an imaginative universe whose inhabitants have lives and journeys of their own.

The little star, just beginning his journey, brings an intimate scale to the vastness of the cosmos. Through such encounters, astronomy and mythology become personal without losing their sense of immensity.

The story may especially interest readers who enjoy the natural world, scientific questions, and fantasies whose magic grows alongside curiosity rather than replacing it.

Beauty and attention

Beauty is a central concern of The Flying Sandolo.

The novel repeatedly invites attention to things that might otherwise be overlooked: the texture of weather, the peculiarities of living creatures, ordinary acts of affection, and the immense scale of the universe.

Its interest in mortality belongs within this larger attention to life. Death and uncertainty are real, but they do not exhaust what the world contains.

The story makes room for delight, fear, curiosity, absurdity, tenderness, and beauty to exist together.

Courage, relationships, and moral imagination

Elyssa's growing courage is not a movement toward fearlessness. It develops through attention, mistakes, investigation, relationships, and action despite uncertainty.

The people around her are imperfect, including those who love her. The story treats generosity and understanding as compatible with disagreement, disappointment, and moral complexity.

Its relationships include friendship, family, human fallibility, and affection for animals whose lives matter beyond their usefulness to the people who love them.

Literary character and reading experience

The Flying Sandolo combines several distinct literary pleasures:

  • Atmospheric storytelling: Close physical description of weather, landscape, food, animals, and the textures of everyday life.
  • Imaginative inhabitation: A detailed fictional world whose routines, relationships, and ordinary pleasures exist alongside the adventure.
  • Coziness without predictability: Warmth, humor, companionship, and familiar rituals within a story that also contains danger, uncertainty, and change.
  • Science-rich fantasy: Natural knowledge and imaginative possibility enriching one another.
  • Generous moral perception: Characters are allowed to be imperfect, contradictory, lovable, and sometimes difficult.
  • Philosophical depth: Questions about mortality, beauty, courage, love, and existence arise through the experiences of a child rather than through prescribed lessons.
  • Respect for the reader: The narrative takes children's intellectual and emotional lives seriously without requiring simple answers to difficult questions.

These qualities may appeal to readers seeking an immersive, beautifully described adventure, a fictional world they can enjoy inhabiting, or imaginative literature that combines tenderness, humor, curiosity, and wonder.

Children, adults, and curious readers

Although written as a children's adventure, the novel offers different points of entry for different readers.

Children may enjoy its flight, mystery, humor, unusual setting, scientific discoveries, friendships, animal characters, and the opportunity to consider enormous questions without being handed simple answers.

For adults, the novel offers questions about parenthood, imperfection, mortality, memory, love, and the limits of adult knowledge.

It also offers the possibility of exoneration: a recognition of the sensitive, earnest, questioning child one once was, and perhaps still is. In a world that often mistakes tenderness for weakness and sincerity for naïveté, the story treats both as expressions of strength and human dignity.

Its generosity extends to people who have learned to be embarrassed by their own capacity to care deeply. The novel makes room for the possibility that these qualities were never deficiencies to outgrow, but valuable ways of being alive.

Readers who do not ordinarily choose fiction may be drawn to its scientific curiosity, detailed environments, ecological imagination, and philosophical concerns.

The book does not require its reader to have experienced bereavement. Nor does it present itself as a therapeutic intervention or a manual for coping with loss.

It is, first, a story: an invitation to adventure, to wonder, and to fuller attention to the experience of being alive.
